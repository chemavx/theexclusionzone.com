Pentagon's Second UAP Declassification: Apollo 12 Astronaut Testimony and Sandia Cold War Records

64 new files, 51 videos, Apollo 12 astronaut testimony, and 209 UAP sightings near nuclear facilities. What the Pentagon's second PURSUE release actually contains.
Share
ChemaVX

A senior intelligence officer described seeing "countless orange orbs swarming in all directions" during a 2025 helicopter mission—a firsthand account now part of the Pentagon's second batch of declassified UAP files released in May 2026, which also includes never-before-public audio from Apollo 12 astronauts describing mysterious "streaks of light" witnessed during their 1969 translunar coast.

A Phased Release Ordered in February 2026

President Donald Trump ordered federal agencies in February 2026 to identify and release records related to unidentified anomalous phenomena (UAP) and potential extraterrestrial material. The Department of Defense, NASA, FBI, and intelligence community elements coordinated a phased declassification process rather than immediate mass release. On May 8, 158 documents — 162 files, counting PDFs, videos and images separately — became available through a new portal called the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE). Two weeks later, on May 22, the Pentagon published an additional batch of 64 files, bringing the archive to 222 documents spanning decades of investigations. Declassifying the strange is not new in Washington: the CIA had already released the archive of Project Stargate, its remote-viewing programme.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth stated that previously classified documents, photographs, and videos of what are officially called "unidentified anomalous phenomena" had long fueled public speculation. The second release on Friday included 51 video files, seven audio recordings, and six PDF documents requested by House lawmakers in March. The All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) located the materials, though officials noted many lacked substantiated chains of custody.

209 Sightings Over Sandia, 1948-1950

A 116-page file documents 209 reported sightings of "green orbs," "discs," and "fireballs" near Sandia National Laboratories in New Mexico between 1948 and 1950. This top-secret facility witnessed unexplained aerial phenomena that investigators could not conclusively identify, with witnesses describing objects maneuvering erratically, vanishing suddenly, or exploding mid-air. The detailed accounts from military personnel at this nuclear weapons-related installation represent one of the most substantial Cold War–era UAP investigations ever made public.

Astronauts aboard Apollo 12 in November 1969 reported seeing "streaks of light" while attempting to sleep during their translunar journey, according to medical debrief recordings released for the first time. Commander Charles Conrad Jr., Lunar Module Pilot Alan L. Bean, and Command Module Pilot Richard F. Gordon Jr. described experiencing flashes despite having their eyes closed in darkness. One crew member stated the streaks appeared "roughly in the same place," while another reported seeing them at approximately "30 degrees to horizontal." NASA later concluded the visual phenomena resulted from cosmic rays affecting the astronauts' retinas rather than external light sources, similar to effects reported by Apollo 11's Buzz Aldrin.

The infrared videos included in the second batch captured military aircraft encounters with unidentified objects between 2018 and 2023, particularly over the Persian Gulf and U.S. Central Command areas. One 2022 video shows multiple spherical objects moving in and out of water near a submarine. A 2021 video labeled "Syrian UAP instant acceleration" depicts an object performing what appears to be instantaneous rapid acceleration—a maneuver that defies known parameters of conventional aviation.

A currently serving senior intelligence officer provided testimony describing a 2025 helicopter mission in which his crew observed "two large orbs" positioned stationary just above the aircraft's rotor disk. The officer stated they were "oval-shaped, orange with a white or yellow center, and emitted light in all directions." He noted that when military jets launched to investigate, the same orbs subsequently appeared to pursue the fighters. The officer concluded his account by writing that the crew "were virtually speechless after these observations."

The release includes historical files from the Department of Energy detailing UFO reports near PANTEX, a key nuclear weapons facility in Texas. Cold War-era documents describe Soviet intelligence activities related to UFO investigations, alongside FBI case files spanning decades. The government website for PURSUE has received over one billion views since the May 8 release, indicating significant public interest.

Apollo Recordings and the Limits of Transparency

The inclusion of Apollo-era astronaut recordings marks a notable shift in government transparency regarding space-based observations. Rather than dismissing historical space program accounts as classified technical data, officials have contextualized them within broader UAP investigations. However, scientists including astrophysicist Neil deGrasse Tyson criticized this approach, noting that NASA files were never classified and astronaut observations typically have rational explanations—pairing them with genuinely unidentified phenomena creates "guilt by association" that inflates the significance of conventional phenomena. The next batch went deeper into the weapons complex: PURSUE Release 4 covered the Pantex intrusion and the green fireballs of Los Alamos.

The Sandia documentation spanning 1948–1950 captures a critical post-World War II period when military installations tracked numerous aerial anomalies. The clustering of 209 incidents at a single sensitive facility suggests either heightened observational attention, genuine anomalous activity, or both. The three-year concentration provides researchers an opportunity to analyze whether sightings correlated with military activities, atmospheric conditions, or geopolitical events.

The 2025 helicopter testimony represents the most recent account released, raising questions about why a contemporary incident would be included in declassification rather than handled through existing reporting channels. The officer's account demonstrates that UAP encounters continue within military operations despite advanced detection systems. The fact that pursuing jets could not identify the objects—standard aircraft in the modern era possess sophisticated identification protocols—suggests either extraordinary stealth capabilities or sensor limitations.

Officials emphasized that documented materials do not contain confirmed evidence of extraterrestrial life or technology. Former AARO director Sean M. Kirkpatrick previously stated that infrasonic camera artifacts, lens effects, and atmospheric phenomena account for most videos when properly analyzed. The Pentagon acknowledged that many recordings lack definitive explanations primarily due to limited data rather than evidence of advanced technology.

The timing of releases appears politically calibrated, with Trump leveraging transparency as a platform achievement while maintaining official skepticism about alien origins. Vice President JD Vance has described himself as "obsessed" with UFO files, while Trump himself expressed ambivalence about extraterrestrial existence when addressing audiences in areas associated with UFO lore like Nevada.

The Most Comprehensive Release, and Still No Answer

The May 2026 Pentagon declassifications represent the most comprehensive government release of historical UAP materials, including credible testimony from astronauts, military personnel, and intelligence officers spanning from Cold War Sandia investigations to contemporary helicopter encounters. While officials have provided no confirmed evidence of extraterrestrial technology, the volume of unresolved cases and the credibility of witnesses demonstrate that anomalous aerial phenomena remain inadequately explained by conventional science.

The Trump administration plans additional releases in the coming months through PURSUE, signaling that the declassification process will continue as records are reviewed and prepared for public distribution.

David Grusch at the Capitol: The Whistleblower Claims, the Congressional Obstruction, and What Comes Next

On June 9, 2026, David Grusch returned to the Capitol steps with four members of Congress to demand what three years of oversight requests have failed to produce. A documented account of the claims, the obstruction, and the unresolved questions that no declassification has yet answered.

Three years after his sworn Congressional testimony, David Grusch returned to Capitol Hill on June 9, 2026 — this time on the steps of the Capitol itself, alongside four members of Congress, to demand what closed-door briefings and oversight requests have so far failed to produce: full declassification of alleged non-human technology programs operating without congressional oversight.

What follows is a documented account of what Grusch has testified, what Congress has been told, and what institutional obstruction has prevented from being confirmed or denied in public settings.

Three Years of Escalating Claims

David Grusch served 14 years as an intelligence officer with the U.S. Air Force and the National Geospatial Intelligence Agency (NGA), reaching GS-15 civilian equivalent status — the equivalent of a full bird colonel. Between 2021 and 2023, he served as his agency's co-lead for UAP and trans-medium object analysis, reporting directly to the UAP Task Force and later to the All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO).

In May 2022, Grusch filed a formal whistleblower complaint through PPD-19 channels with the Intelligence Community Inspector General, citing concerns about government operations with "secrecy above congressional oversight" regarding UAPs. The Inspector General determined his complaint was "urgent and credible" for the intelligence committees.

On July 26, 2023, Grusch provided sworn testimony before Congress — the first time a credentialed intelligence official had done so on record regarding alleged crash retrieval programs. The June 9, 2026 Capitol press conference marked the three-year anniversary of that testimony, with Grusch joined by Representatives Tim Burchett, Anna Paulina Luna, Eric Burlison, and Jared Moskowitz.

What Grusch Has Testified Under Oath

Grusch testified that he personally interviewed over 40 witnesses with direct knowledge of crash retrieval programs spanning multiple decades. He stated his assessment — based on those interviews — is that the U.S. government possesses intact non-human spacecraft, recovered materials, and biological remains from multiple incidents.

On timeline, Grusch identified the 1930s as when the government first became aware of non-human intelligence evidence, citing Italian government documentation from 1933 describing recovery of a partially intact vehicle near Magenta, Italy. He referenced the 1947 Roswell incident, calling the Air Force's official debunking report "a total hack job" and stating that multiple eyewitnesses had observed recovered bodies.

Regarding nuclear weapons facilities, Grusch described a documented 1967 incident at Malmstrom Air Force Base in Montana, where craft appearing intelligently controlled hovered above nuclear missile silos, resulting in the temporary shutdown of ten ICBMs. He described multiple instances of UAPs exhibiting sustained interest in American nuclear technology — a pattern also documented in declassified records now publicly available through the PURSUE portal.

On biological remains, Grusch stated under oath that non-human biologics accompanied some crash recoveries, based on assessments from people with direct knowledge still participating in the programs. He declined to provide photographic or videographic evidence in public settings, citing classification restrictions, but indicated such evidence exists and offered to brief Congress in secure compartmentalized facilities (SCIFs).

The Program Structure: How Oversight Was Allegedly Bypassed

According to Grusch's testimony, the programs operate through special access programs (SAPs) and unacknowledged special access programs (USAPs) that have evaded proper congressional oversight. He provided names of specific programs to congressional intelligence committees and the Inspector General but stated he could not name them publicly without jeopardizing national security. The name that keeps surfacing in that structure is Immaculate Constellation, a programme Congress has never been able to confirm.

Grusch testified these programs operate above normal congressional notification requirements. While 10 U.S. Code Section 119 mandates that the "Gang of Eight" receive notification of unacknowledged SAPs, Grusch indicated this safeguard has been circumvented through mechanisms he described as "abuse."

On funding, Grusch described how defense contractors overcharge for conventional technology, with excess funds diverted to classified programs through Internal Research and Development (IR&D) funds — money that defense contractors control without transparent congressional notification requirements. He confirmed private aerospace contractors are directly involved in reverse engineering efforts and material storage, though declined to publicly identify which corporations.

Congressional Access Restrictions

Members of Congress expressed consistent frustration about access restrictions throughout both the 2023 hearing and the June 2026 press conference. Representatives Burchett and Luna testified they were turned away at Eglin Air Force Base when attempting to investigate a reported UAP incident involving four craft in diamond formation detected by radar. When given access to one image captured by flight crew, the image reportedly showed a large floating orb not matching any known human capability.

Congressman Ogles proposed invoking the Holman Rule to force agency compliance: "If that access is denied I will personally volunteer to initiate the Holman Rule against any personnel or any program or any agency that denies access to Congress."

Representative Burlison stated at the June 9 press conference: "President Trump is the first president to take concrete steps towards transparency on UAP. The recent releases are an important start, so now it's the obligation of Congress to make sure career intelligence officials don't obstruct his directive, in order to prevent selective disclosure or another dead end."

Corroborating Military Testimony

Grusch's claims are supported — though not confirmed — by testimony from military aviators with direct observational experience. Former Navy pilot Ryan Graves described specific UAP encounters involving dark gray or black cubes inside clear spheres, hovering stationary in hurricane-force winds before accelerating to Mach 1.1 to 1.2. Multiple pilots came within 50 feet of these objects without observing visible propulsion systems, control surfaces, or thermal signatures. Graves estimated that only approximately 5 percent of UAP sightings are reported, with 95 percent remaining undocumented due to professional stigma. The most heavily documented retrieval claim outside the United States is the 1996 Varginha case in Brazil, which a congressional hearing reopened in 2025.

Commander David Fravor, who encountered the Tic Tac object on November 14, 2004 — the incident that eventually forced the Pentagon to acknowledge UAP — testified that the craft exhibited acceleration from zero to matching his F-18's velocity instantaneously, with no visible propulsion or control surfaces, and demonstrated the ability to disable radar systems when approached. That case is documented in full in our investigation of the USS Nimitz 2004 encounter.

The Kirkpatrick Contradiction

The most direct institutional conflict in Grusch's testimony involves Dr. Sean Kirkpatrick, former director of AARO. Kirkpatrick testified in April 2023 that his office found "no credible evidence thus far of extraterrestrial activity or off-world technology" — a position consistent with AARO's comprehensive 2024 report, which we examined in detail in our investigation of eight decades of U.S. government UAP research.

Grusch directly contradicted this, stating Kirkpatrick received approximately 30 individuals with direct knowledge and that Grusch personally evaluated information these individuals provided to the director. Grusch expressed concern that despite briefing Kirkpatrick on specific findings a year prior to his AARO appointment, the director never followed up. The gap between what AARO publicly concluded and what Grusch claims was available to it remains one of the central unresolved questions in the disclosure debate.

Obstruction and Whistleblower Retaliation

Grusch characterized suppression tactics as "administrative terrorism," explaining how career government officials use security clearance threats and professional retaliation to silence anyone with knowledge of the programs. He personally experienced retaliation and noted an active whistleblower reprisal investigation on his behalf at the time of his 2023 testimony.

The 2019 UAP Security Classification Guidelines — which Grusch helped author — have since been used to prevent disclosure. Pilots reported receiving cease-and-desist letters from commercial airline corporations for discussing UAP safety concerns. The systematic chilling effect on reporting is documented: Graves's estimate that 95 percent of sightings go unreported represents not a data gap but an institutional design.

What Remains Unresolved

Despite three years of escalating congressional interest, fundamental questions remain unanswered in public settings. Grusch declined to discuss whether the government has made actual contact with intelligent extraterrestrial life, stating this "cannot be discussed in a public setting." He declined to provide specific documentation of biological remains beyond confirming their existence based on witness assessments. Disclosure moved elsewhere, if not here: the second Pentagon declassification, ordered in February 2026, released 209 sightings over Sandia from 1948 to 1950.

NASA's independent UAP study team, despite extensive access to government archives, did not receive comprehensive briefings on the alleged programs. Congressional investigators expressed frustration that relevant agencies have not substantiated Grusch's claims through official channels — yet have simultaneously restricted access to the classified information where such substantiation allegedly exists.

The 222 declassified files released through the PURSUE portal in May 2026 — the most comprehensive government disclosure in American history — explicitly state that the released materials do not confirm extraterrestrial life or recovered alien technology. Whether that position reflects the complete picture of what the government holds, or represents the limit of what has been authorized for release, is precisely the question the June 9 press conference was designed to force into the open. That full analysis is available in our investigation of the 222 declassified UAP files.

Three Years On, Neither Confirmed Nor Refuted

The June 9, 2026 Capitol Hill press conference marked three years of Grusch's claims moving from classified channels to public record — without the government either confirming or definitively refuting them. The credibility of the witnesses, the pattern of congressional access restrictions, and the direct contradiction between Grusch's testimony and AARO's public conclusions collectively define an institutional accountability problem that declassification alone may not resolve.

The path forward requires one of three outcomes: immediate declassification of the specific files Grusch has identified by name to oversight bodies; comprehensive closed-door congressional briefings with unrestricted access; or formal investigations into potential obstruction of congressional oversight. What June 9 demonstrated is that three years after the first sworn testimony, none of those three outcomes has materialized — and the pressure to produce one is now coming from both Congress and the White House simultaneously.

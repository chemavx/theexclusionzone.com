Brazil's 1996 Varginha Incident: When a Small Town Allegedly Captured Non-Human Beings

Three girls, a dying soldier, a neurosurgeon who stayed silent for 30 years, and a Congressional hearing in 2026. The most documented alleged alien contact event in the Western Hemisphere.
Share
ChemaVX

On January 20, 1996, the unremarkable municipality of Varginha, nestled in the coffee-growing region of Minas Gerais, Brazil, became the epicenter of what many consider the Western Hemisphere's most thoroughly documented alleged extraterrestrial contact event — a case so significant it has become known as "Brazil's Roswell." Unlike the mythology surrounding the original 1947 Roswell incident, which unfolded decades before widespread video documentation, Varginha's story emerged in the age of instantaneous media, captured in real-time interviews, and has only intensified as new witnesses have stepped forward in recent years—most notably at a January 2026 Washington, D.C. press conference that brought the case directly to Congress.

The Initial Sightings: A Week of Escalating Anomalies

The Varginha narrative does not begin abruptly on January 20. Instead, a sequence of sightings over the preceding week suggests a pattern of sustained activity in the region. On January 13, Carlos de Souza, a geography teacher and ultralight pilot, reported witnessing a cylindrical or submarine-shaped craft trailing smoke and descending erratically near the town. Military personnel allegedly arrived within minutes and ordered him away at gunpoint after he picked up a fragment of material that, when crushed in his fist, immediately returned to its original shape—a detail that would resurface in later accounts. De Souza did not publicly discuss the incident for decades, claiming he was subsequently confronted by men in an unmarked vehicle who threatened his family.

In the early hours of January 20, at approximately 1 a.m., farmers Eurico de Freitas and his partner Oralina Augusta de Freitas watched from their property as a large gray, cigar-shaped object moved silently at low altitude over their pasture. Both described the object as roughly the size of a bus, trailing bright smoke. Oralina's initial observation was triggered by sudden panic among their cattle. The couple observed the craft for roughly forty minutes before it vanished. Their description matched a drawing made six days earlier by a young girl in a separate sighting, suggesting visual continuity rather than isolated misidentifications.

The Firefighters' Morning Encounter: An Unidentified "Animal"

Around 8 a.m. on January 20, an anonymous caller contacted Varginha's fire department requesting assistance with a mysterious "animal" in the Jardim Andere district. Firefighters arrived approximately two hours later and encountered a small, roughly four-foot-tall bipedal creature with oily brown skin, a disproportionately large head, prominent red eyes described as "two red balls," and three raised protuberances on its forehead. The being appeared unsteady and emitted a faint buzzing sound. According to multiple witness accounts, including a builder's assistant watching from a nearby rooftop, four firefighters netted the creature, placed it in a wooden box, and handed it to soldiers from the Escola de Sargentos das Armas (EsSA) military academy based in nearby Três Corações, approximately forty kilometers away. The entire operation reportedly took less than an hour before military vehicles departed the scene.

The Three Girls and the Afternoon Encounter

The case's most vivid and widely documented account emerged at approximately 3:30 p.m., when three young women—sisters Liliane Fátima Silva (16) and Valquíria Aparecida Silva (14), and their friend Kátia Andrade Xavier (21)—were walking home from work through the Jardim Andere neighborhood. They took a shortcut through a vacant lot and encountered a crouched figure approximately twenty-five feet away on Dr. Benevenuto Braz Vieira Street. The creature matched earlier descriptions: small stature, large head, oily brown skin with vein-like patterns, enormous red eyes, three small horns or protuberances, and an acrid odor. The girls' reaction was visceral—Liliane screamed and told her mother she had seen "the devil." When the girls' mother and their friend returned to the location within thirty minutes, they found only a lingering ammonia-like smell and an unusual three-toed footprint.

A mason working nearby told investigators that firefighters had already captured "that weird animal." The consistency of these accounts would later become the case's greatest strength: over nearly three decades, the three young women maintained identical descriptions without deviation, refusing opportunities to recant despite social ostracism in their hometown.

The Hospital Handoff and Medical Mystery

Accounts from military sources, many speaking anonymously with distorted voices on tape, alleged that a second creature was transported to the Regional Hospital of Varginha and subsequently transferred to the better-equipped Humanitas Hospital. On January 22, between 3 and 6 p.m., multiple witnesses reported three military trucks positioned outside Humanitas. Hospital staff allegedly observed an isolated ward sealed off, accompanied by an acrid ammonia smell that persisted for weeks.

In November 2025—nearly thirty years later—Dr. Ítalo Venturelli, the head neurosurgeon at Regional Hospital, came forward publicly for the first time. He testified before closed-door Congressional meetings and at the National Press Club that he had been called to examine a patient brought through a back entrance with severe cranial trauma. Venturelli described brief but profound contact with a living being he assessed clinically as non-human: white-colored skin under hospital lighting, teardrop-shaped eyes of lilac color, a small mouth, minimal ear structure, and a body temperature he found unusual. He estimated the encounter lasted three to four minutes. Venturelli stated the being communicated through its eyes—not telepathically in the traditional sense, but through a transmission of "calm and tranquility" that he interpreted as a far greater intelligence than his own. He described looking at the creature, the creature looking at him, the creature then gazing out the window at the blue sky, then returning its gaze to him—as if expressing a desire for freedom. Armed personnel then burst into the room, removed the patient, and warned staff not to speak.

Venturelli attributed his three-decade silence to fear of professional ridicule. He came forward only after suffering a near-fatal heart attack, which prompted him to reconsider his remaining years.

The Death of Marco Eli Chereze: Sepsis or Something Else

The case's darkest and most medically contentious element involves Military Police Corporal Marco Eli Chereze, age twenty-three. According to multiple sources, Chereze participated in capturing one of the creatures with his bare hands and transported it. Within days, he developed a small abscess under his armpit requiring surgical drainage. His condition deteriorated rapidly. By February 12, he was hospitalized with fever and severe pain throughout his body. On February 15, less than a month after the alleged encounter, he died from generalized sepsis and respiratory failure.

The official death certificate listed the cause as post-surgical bacterial infection—a straightforward medical conclusion. However, his widow, Valeria Chereze, later reported being denied complete medical records for months, and when documents finally arrived, pages were missing. She stated her husband had perfect health and never even caught colds before this incident. Moreover, Chereze allegedly became nervous whenever the ET case was mentioned in his presence and appeared to be hiding something.

In 2025, two forensic pathologists and a lab director with knowledge of Chereze's case made extraordinary claims on camera. Dr. João Janini, a pathological anatomist with a biography stating he had performed over fifty thousand autopsies, provided a signed statement to Congressional representatives claiming that he had identified a bacterium in tissue samples from Chereze of "extremely high aggressiveness and lethality" that exhibited characteristics "so far beyond the limits of what is conventional" that he raised "the hypothesis of its alien origin." The treating physician, Dr. Cesário Lincoln Furtado, was quoted at the time as saying he had administered "the best antibiotics we had" with no effect—"it was like giving water with sugar." The official medical record has never been released for independent analysis.

The Zoo Deaths and Environmental Contamination

In the weeks following January 20, Leila Cabra, the biologist managing Varginha's municipal zoo, reported that several animals died under unusual circumstances. According to her and the zoo veterinarian (now the zoo's director), approximately five animals died between February and April, including a tapir, an ocelot, a spider monkey, and two species of gray brockets (small deer). All exhibited identical symptoms despite being different species with different diets housed in different zoo sections.

Necropsies revealed severe blackening of the stomach lining and intestinal mucosa. Samples sent to a laboratory in Belo Horizonte were reported to contain "an unidentified toxic-caustic substance." The veterinarian confirmed these findings and stated that the uniform presentation across unrelated species was medically inexplicable through conventional means.

Additionally, Leila reported that firefighters had approached her on January 20 requesting she accept delivery of "a very weird animal," which she declined. When she visited the zoo after the storm, no creature was present.

Military Operations and Hospital Movements

In the days and weeks following January 20, residents and medical staff reported unusual concentrations of military and emergency vehicles surrounding both Regional Hospital and Humanitas Hospital. Army trucks, fire department vehicles, and police cruisers were observed conducting what appeared to be coordinated operations. Military officials provided contradictory explanations—one involved the exhumation of a body requiring extensive forensic personnel and official ceremony; another involved the childbirth of a "midget woman." These explanations struck investigators as implausible given the scale and duration of the operations.

Anonymous military sources, later interviewed by ufologists with voice distortion, claimed that a wooden casket containing a small, humanoid body was present in an isolated hospital ward surrounded by approximately fifteen doctors, firefighters, and soldiers. One account described a medical professional using forceps to extract a black tongue from the creature's mouth, which allegedly snapped back elastically when released. Observers noted three long fingers, no visible sexual organs, no navel, shiny brown skin with abraded knees, and a strong ammonia odor. On the evening of January 22, the casket was reportedly wrapped in black plastic and loaded into a truck that joined a pre-dawn convoy toward Campinas.

The Alleged Campinas Transfer and American Involvement

Multiple accounts, including testimony provided to filmmaker James Fox, suggested that the being(s) were transported to the University of Campinas (Unicamp) for analysis. Beyond that point, allegations diverge into claims of American involvement. A Brazilian air traffic controller, Marco Feres, reportedly informed investigators that a U.S. Air Force cargo aircraft—likely a C-17 or C-5—landed at Viracopos Airport in Campinas on or around January 20-22, 1996, to collect an unusual cargo. Fox's team submitted a records request to the U.S. government for flight manifests and landing permits covering January 13-27, 1996. The response, Fox claims, was a classified denial—the agency would neither confirm nor deny the existence of such records.

In his January 2026 National Press Club presentation, retired U.S. Air Force Colonel Fred Claussen outlined the documentary trail such a mission would necessarily create: paperwork from Air Mobility Command at Scott Air Force Base in Illinois, refueling records, international flight plans, and documentation from the Air Mobility Wing at Charleston Air Force Base in South Carolina. He emphasized that even classified missions require extensive administrative footprints and that thirty to forty Americans involved in such an operation would possess direct knowledge.

Witness Intimidation and the Offer

Approximately four months after the initial sighting, the three young women reported being visited at their home by five men dressed in dark clothing around 10 p.m. The men allegedly offered a briefcase containing a substantial sum of money—enough, according to the girls, to "leave Brazil"—in exchange for recording televised statements denying their encounter and describing the creature as a sick animal, a sick dog, or a sick human. The girls' mother, Luiza Helena da Silva, refused and reported feeling very frightened.

The girls also reported experiencing sustained harassment: mysterious phone calls, surveillance, and social persecution in their hometown. They were mocked as opportunists, questioned about their motives, and faced job rejections attributed to their association with the case. Liliane dropped out of school. Kátia, who was pregnant at the time, suffered severe depression and eventually divorced her husband after people suggested to her that she might be carrying a "creature baby." Valquíria eventually withdrew from public discussion of the incident entirely, stating the case had brought nothing but suffering to her and her sister.

Yet over nearly thirty years, they never recanted. Their account remained consistent in every documented interview.

Investigators in Mississippi tried to put that kind of consistency to the test in 1973, when they left the Pascagoula fishermen Charles Hickson and Calvin Parker alone in an interview room with a hidden recorder running. The tape, read closely, settles less than most accounts claim.

The Official Military Investigation and the "Mudinho" Explanation

In May 1996, the Escola de Sargentos das Armas initiated an internal review in response to growing media coverage and rumors of military involvement. This evolved into Military Police Inquiry (IPM) No. 18/1997, a formal investigation conducted by the Superior Military Court. The investigation's two volumes, totaling approximately six hundred pages, were eventually digitized and made publicly accessible through Brazil's ARQUIMEDES archive.

The same archive holds the Air Force's own file on Operação Prato, the 1977 investigation into the beams of light that burned villagers on the island of Colares — the most complete military record of a UAP investigation any government has released.

The official conclusion dismissed the entire incident as resulting from misidentification and routine military activity. The creature allegedly seen by the three girls was identified as Luís Antônio de Paula, nicknamed "Mudinho" (meaning "mute" or "silent"), a local man with mental and physical disabilities who was often seen crouching or walking in an unusual posture around the city. The investigation presented photographs of Mudinho as evidence of visual similarity.

Military vehicle movements were explained as scheduled maintenance at a Mercedes dealership in Varginha, supported by maintenance work orders and invoices that the Army cited as documentation. The fire department's activities were attributed to routine animal control responses during severe weather. All claims regarding creature capture, hospital isolation, and military operations were dismissed as media-amplified rumors arising from storm-induced mass hysteria and coincidental events.

The investigation concluded: "The military personnel cited by the press did not participate in any operation transporting any type of cargo. The media are mistaken, publicizing untrue events."

When confronted with the "Mudinho" theory, the three young women rejected it categorically. Valquíria stated in a 2016 documentary interview: "We had known Mudinho since we were children; he was always crouching low. Without a doubt, it wasn't him."

The 2025 Congressional Testimony: A Case Reopened

Thirty years after the incident, the Varginha case re-emerged in American politics with unprecedented momentum. On January 15, 2026, filmmaker James Fox arranged a closed-door meeting with three Congressional representatives—Tim Burchett (R-TN), Anna Paulina Luna (R-FL), and Eric Burlison (R-MO)—in Burchett's office in Longworth House Office Building. Present were three Brazilian witnesses: Dr. Ítalo Venturelli (the neurosurgeon), Carlos de Souza (the geography teacher and pilot who witnessed the initial craft), and Dr. Armando Fortunato (a forensic pathologist with over thirty years of experience in criminal examination).

Venturelli provided detailed testimony regarding his encounter with the living being at the hospital, describing its appearance, its apparent intelligence, and its non-verbal communication. De Souza recounted his experience on January 13, the craft's appearance, the ammonia-like odor, the metallic fragment that returned to its original shape, and the armed confrontation with military personnel. Dr. Armando described his autopsy of Marco Chereze and presented a signed statement from the renowned pathologist Dr. João Janini attesting to the unprecedented bacterial characteristics found in tissue samples.

Five days later, on January 20, 2026—exactly thirty years to the day after the initial sightings—Fox organized a public press conference at the National Press Club in Washington, D.C. The event drew Congressional interest, media attention, and participation from Kirk McConnell, a former Senate and House intelligence committee staffer with thirty-seven years of experience. McConnell testified that the Senate Intelligence Committee had received reports similar to those from Varginha from multiple credible sources, and that classified meetings had been conducted with "very credible sources reporting both direct and secondhand knowledge of the reality of highly intelligent nonhuman beings, government retrievals and reverse engineering of craft not made by human beings, and the recovery of bodies of non-human beings."

Brazilian Senator Eduardo Girão, representing the state of Ceará and who had previously led a Brazilian Senate hearing on UAP in 2022, attended in a private capacity and met with Congressional representatives to discuss potential joint investigative efforts to locate documentation and physical evidence.

The Case's Evolving Dimensions: New Witnesses and Conflicting Narratives

Recent years have witnessed both reinforcement and complication of the Varginha narrative. In addition to Dr. Venturelli's 2025 testimony, other medical professionals have come forward. A former regional police chief, speaking to Fox, reported observing a tarp-covered non-human body at the ESA military base amid unusual helicopter traffic and an extraordinary concentration of senior Brazilian officers.

Simultaneously, skeptical voices have intensified. In a 2025 documentary series, "The Mystery of Varginha," original investigator Ubirajara Rodrigues—the lawyer who first interviewed the three girls—confessed that the case was "built on assumptions, untruths and general nonsense." A former soldier who had claimed military capture of an alien being admitted to Fox's producers that he had fabricated his account after being offered a substantial bribe, calling the entire military cover-up narrative "one of the biggest farces ever."

These contradictions highlight a central challenge in evaluating the case: the absence of physical evidence—photographs, video, biological samples, or recovered material—that could be independently verified. No hospital records have been released documenting unusual patient intake or treatment. The alleged video footage of a captured creature, reportedly held by individuals too frightened to release it, has never materialized despite Fox's offer of a $200,000 reward. The Chereze autopsy records and tissue samples have never been made publicly available for independent analysis.

Cultural Aftermath: UFO Tourism and Municipal Identity

Regardless of the case's truth, its cultural impact has been profound and economically significant. Varginha has transformed itself into Brazil's "UFO capital," investing heavily in themed infrastructure. A twenty-meter-tall spaceship-shaped water tower, painted purple and illuminated at night, dominates the city's central park. City Hall features a spaceship-shaped elevator. Bus stops designed as flying saucers transport residents through town. In September 2025, a thirteen-foot-tall alien statue created by artist Renato Criaturas was installed near the city's ET Memorial.

Tourism has generated substantial revenue. According to Tourism Secretary Rosana Carvalho, approximately two hundred thousand visitors from nearly forty countries—including New Zealand and Japan—have visited since the ET Museum opened in 2022. A planned larger museum complex received federal funding but languished incomplete, leaving what residents describe as a "rusty skeleton of a spaceship surrounded by weeds" that now shelters stray dogs. American investors have explored developing a themed attraction comparable to the Loch Ness Monster tourism industry in Scotland.

This commercialization has created an ethical tension: the original witnesses have been approached by media outlets and investigators offering compensation for interviews. One of the three girls, when approached by the Guardian newspaper in 2024, replied to interview requests: "If you're paying a fee I can talk to the girls... I'm very busy, thanks OK."

Evidence, Interpretation, and Institutional Response

The Varginha case presents competing categories of evidence. What is definitively verified: Military Police Inquiry No. 18/1997 exists and is publicly archived with documented metadata; Globo's Fantástico broadcast interviews with the three girls in February 1996; Marco Eli Chereze died on February 15 from documented sepsis following a minor procedure. What is probable based on convergent witness testimony: multiple independent civilians reported low-altitude or ground-level anomalies across a two-day window; a cylindrical craft on January 13 and a disc performing a methodical grid search on January 20; hospital activity that deviated from routine.

What remains disputed and hotly contested: whether any non-human being was captured or held in military custody; whether the being seen by the three girls was actually Mudinho; the true etiology of Marco Chereze's fatal infection; and whether American forces took custody of remains. What is legend or cultural interpretation: religious framings of the being as "devil-like" or "angelic," reflecting witness psychology under acute stress rather than objective description.

The case's analytical weakness is profound: the complete absence of independently recoverable physical evidence—biological material, photographs, authenticated video, medical records, or documentation. The Brazilian military's investigation provided plausible alternative explanations for every element. The claim of a U.S. cargo plane operation at Campinas lacks documented support in accessible aviation records. The alleged video footage of the entities has never surfaced despite three decades of opportunity and financial incentives.

Yet the case's investigative strength lies in the convergence of testimony from dozens of independent witnesses, the professional credentials of researchers and medical professionals willing to go on record, and the unusual transparency of the Brazilian military in publishing a detailed public investigation rather than maintaining indefinite secrecy.

Verified Facts, Contested Meaning

The Varginha UFO incident remains fundamentally unresolved. Whether January 20, 1996, witnessed an extraterrestrial contact event or an elaborate example of how ordinary events can be mythologized and amplified through media and collective psychology, the case will likely never achieve definitive closure without additional documentary or physical evidence. What is certain is that something extraordinary captured the Brazilian public imagination, transformed a small coffee-growing town into an international point of pilgrimage for UFO researchers, and generated sufficient witness testimony—much of it consistent over three decades—to ensure its permanent place in both ufological literature and the broader conversation about the possibility of non-human intelligence visiting Earth. As Congressional interest in UAP matters continues to grow, Varginha represents a test case for how international cooperation, declassification of records, and systematic investigation of historical incidents might either resolve or deepen the mystery of what occurred in this Brazilian municipality thirty years ago.

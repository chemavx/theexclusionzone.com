Chile's Military Declassified UAP Files: CEFAA's Groundbreaking Investigation of Unexplained Aerial Phenomena

Chile's CEFAA investigated a November 2014 Navy helicopter encounter with an unidentified aerial object captured on thermal footage. Despite rigorous multidisciplinary analysis, the object defied all conventional explanations—yet Chile published the complete video and reports publicly.

On November 11, 2014, a Chilean Navy helicopter filmed an unidentified object for nine minutes and twelve seconds using a sophisticated infrared camera. Two radar stations saw nothing. Air traffic control had authorized no aircraft in the area. After two years of analysis by a multidisciplinary scientific committee, Chile's official UAP investigation agency published the complete footage and its conclusion: "We do not know what it was, but we do know what it was not."

CEFAA: The Agency That Chose Transparency

The Committee for the Study of Anomalous Aerial Phenomena (CEFAA), now reorganized as SEFAA (Sección de Estudios de Fenómenos Aéreos Anómalos), was officially established on October 3, 1997, under Chile's General Directorate of Civil Aeronautics (DGAC) — Chile's equivalent of the FAA. Its roots trace back to 1968 and a commission that operated until 1975 following notable sightings near Arica.

CEFAA's founding philosophy was rooted in aviation safety, not extraterrestrial research. The agency explicitly rejects the term "UFO," preferring "UAP" or the Spanish "FANI" (Fenómenos Aéreos No Identificados), deliberately distancing itself from ufological speculation. Operating across territories spanning 32 million square kilometers — from Arica to the South Pole — the agency's primary concern has always been whether unexplained objects pose risks to civilian and military aviation.

What distinguishes CEFAA from every comparable government body is its publication policy: completed investigations are released publicly in full, including video footage, radar data, witness statements, and expert analysis. The 2014 Navy helicopter case is the clearest example of what that policy produces.

The November 2014 Helicopter Incident

On November 11, 2014, a Chilean Navy Airbus Cougar AS-532 conducted routine daytime patrol along the coast west of Santiago at approximately 4,500 feet altitude. Aboard were a highly experienced Navy Captain pilot and a Navy technician operating a WESCAM MX-15 HD Forward Looking Infrared (FLIR) camera — equipment designed for medium-altitude covert intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance. The helicopter maintained approximately 132 knots under clear afternoon conditions with unlimited horizontal visibility.

At 1:52 p.m., the technician observed a strange object flying to the left over the ocean. Both men identified it visually and estimated it maintained comparable velocity and altitude to the helicopter, positioned approximately 35 to 40 miles distant, traveling in a west-northwest direction. Neither of two nearby radar stations — including the main DGAC Ground Primary Radar system in Santiago — detected the object despite it being well within detection range. Air traffic controllers confirmed no civilian or military aircraft had been authorized in the controlled airspace where the object appeared.

The technician filmed continuously for nine minutes and twelve seconds, predominantly in infrared mode. Two thermal anomalies defined the footage: the object itself, described by the Captain as a "flat, elongated structure" with "two thermal spotlights like discharges that did not coincide with the axel of motion," and two separate ejections of highly thermal material creating massive plumes that trailed behind the object before blending into clouds.

Two Years of Investigation: What the Committee Ruled Out

Following standard Navy protocol, footage was immediately transferred to CEFAA. General Ricardo Bermúdez, then CEFAA Director, and nuclear chemist Mario Avila personally interviewed both officers at their Navy base. Avila reported being "very impressed by these witnesses," noting they were "highly trained professionals with many years experience, and they were absolutely certain that they could not explain what they saw."

Over approximately two years, at least eight contentious meetings convened CEFAA's scientific committee. Specialists included astrophysicist Luis Barrera, Air Force photogrammetric image experts, French video analysts from France's GEIPAN agency, meteorologist Luis Salazar, DGAC aeronautic engineers, and digital imaging specialists from Santiago's National Museum of Aeronautics and Space. Complete radar data, satellite weather information, and detailed air traffic records were examined.

French analysts initially proposed the object was a medium-haul aircraft approaching Santiago airport, with the thermal discharges representing dumped cabin waste water. Chilean experts definitively rejected this: radar would have detected any landing aircraft; landing clearance would have been required; standard protocol demands permission before ejecting any material; and the pilots would have recognized an aircraft. NASA data confirms contrails form only at cruise altitudes above 26,000 feet in temperatures below -40°C — not during landing approaches at 4,500 feet in 50°F ambient conditions. Any expelled water would have immediately fallen to ground level.

The committee also ruled out birds, space debris, insects, unauthorized aircraft, skydivers, hang-gliders, atmospheric phenomena, weather balloons, and drones. The formal conclusion: "The great majority of committee members agreed to call the subject in question a UAP due to the number of highly researched reasons that it was unanimously agreed could not explain it."

What the Infrared Footage Actually Showed

The infrared recording revealed details inaccessible through visible-light observation. The object's thermal signature appeared white against the dark ocean — indicating the object was colder than the surrounding sea surface. This directly contradicts any heat-producing propulsion system. The thermal properties of the ejected material suggested a gas or energy discharge, not a liquid substance.

In infrared mode, the large thermal plume created by the discharges blended seamlessly into clouds — rendering it virtually invisible to unequipped observers in visible light. Without the FLIR camera's specific capabilities, the event would have passed completely unnoticed despite occurring in daylight in controlled airspace.

CEFAA International Affairs Director Jose Lay characterized the case's significance: "It is our first video taken with a sophisticated camera in the infrared; the first time we have ever seen the ejection of a substance from a UAP; the first time we have a sighting lasting over nine minutes with two highly reliable witnesses."

The Collahuasi Mine Case: A Supporting Data Point

Before the 2014 Navy incident, CEFAA investigated a significant case in April 2013 at Chile's Collahuasi copper mine — one of the world's largest copper operations. Four engineers observed a "flattened disc of brilliant color with a diameter of 5 to 10 meters," performing ascending, descending, and horizontal movements at approximately 600 meters altitude. One engineer photographed the object.

The committee eliminated meteorological phenomena, experimental aircraft, weather balloons, and drones. Lay noted that fishing companies operating near the site used drones extensively, and "this was definitely not a drone" — citing the absence of characteristic noise. The investigation concluded: "It is an object or phenomenon of great interest, and it can be qualified as a UFO." Yet reluctance from direct witnesses to participate underscored a recurring obstacle: reporting stigma that limits investigative depth.

CEFAA as a Global Model — and Chile in the 2026 Context

NASA's 2023 Independent Study Team Report identified data standardization as the critical barrier to scientific UAP understanding — the lack of proper sensor calibration, comprehensive metadata, and rigorous collection protocols. CEFAA's approach represents precisely the methodology NASA recommends: calibrated instruments, complete contextual data, and multidisciplinary expert analysis released publicly upon completion.

Current director Hugo Camus, leading SEFAA since 2017, acknowledges that approximately 66 percent of cases achieve conventional explanation, while 4-5 percent remain unexplained due to limitations in current scientific understanding. That 4-5 percent — from a scientifically rigorous agency that eliminates the obvious before publishing — is a very different number than 4-5 percent from an agency with lower methodological standards.

Chile's model is now being cited internationally. The third PURSUE release in June 2026 — covered in our full investigation of the 294 declassified files — explicitly noted that France's GEIPAN, Chile's SEFAA, and Canada's newly proposed UAP reporting system represent the international framework the U.S. is now moving toward, however unevenly. The contrast remains stark: Chile published a nine-minute infrared video and two years of expert analysis in 2014. The U.S. released three compressed clips in 2020 and continues to classify the radar data that would contextualize them.

A Different Model for Investigating the Unexplained

Chile's CEFAA/SEFAA represents a distinct model for institutional UAP investigation — transparent, scientifically rigorous, and explicitly concerned with aviation safety rather than extraterrestrial speculation. The 2014 Navy helicopter footage demonstrates that unexplained phenomena can be documented with high-quality sensors, comprehensive metadata, and expert analysis, yet still resist categorical explanation within current scientific frameworks.

General Bermúdez's summary remains the most precise statement any government body has offered on a UAP case: "We do not know what it was, but we do know what it was not." After two years of investigation, nine minutes of thermal footage, two credible military witnesses, and the unanimous conclusion of a multidisciplinary scientific committee, that is still the most accurate description of what happened off the Chilean coast on November 11, 2014 — and arguably, the most honest position any government has taken on unexplained aerial phenomena anywhere in the world.

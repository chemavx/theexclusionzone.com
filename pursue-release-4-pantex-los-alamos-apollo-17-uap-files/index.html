PURSUE Release 4: The Pantex Intrusion, the Green Fireballs of Los Alamos, and a New Look at Apollo 17

The Pentagon's fourth PURSUE release includes a 2015 nuclear facility intrusion in Texas, a declassified 1949 Los Alamos conference where atomic scientists couldn't explain recurring "green fireballs," and new government analysis of an Apollo 17 photograph.

ChemaVX

The Department of War released its fourth tranche of declassified Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP) files on July 10, 2026, continuing the Trump administration's rolling transparency effort. The latest disclosure includes 40 files — 14 documents, 19 videos, four audio recordings, and three images — sourced from the Pentagon, NASA, CIA, FBI, and Department of Energy. The collection joins the growing archive at WAR.GOV/UFO, with Pentagon Chief Spokesman Sean Parnell confirming the release as part of the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE).

Four Releases in Nine Weeks

The first batch launched May 8 with 158 records in 162 files, covered in our initial analysis of the PURSUE launch. The second arrived May 22 with 64 more, taking the archive to 222, examined in our breakdown of the Apollo astronaut testimony and Sandia Cold War records. The third landed June 12 with 72 files, including the CIA's 1992 confession about U-2 spy planes explaining decades of misattributed sightings — covered here in full. This fourth release adds 40 more, bringing the archive to 334 records across four tranches, with WAR.GOV/UFO drawing over 1.7 billion hits worldwide since May 8. The Department of War says it remains "actively working on the next release," with further tranches expected on a rolling basis.

A Six-Pointed Star Over the Yellow Sea

Among the release's most distinctive footage, military infrared sensors captured an object over the Yellow Sea described as resembling a six-pointed star formation with alternating arm lengths — a geometry not reported in any of the previous three tranches. A separate video tracks an unidentified object over the East China Sea for several minutes. Both incidents occurred in 2025 under Indo-Pacific Command operations. The footage shows sensor degradation consistent with electromagnetic interference, a signature flagged in earlier releases as characteristic of certain UAP encounters. Military observers noted the objects' flight characteristics lacked explanation under known aircraft parameters.

The Pantex Intrusion: Another Nuclear Facility on the List

An Energy Department file details an intrusion event at the Pantex nuclear weapons facility near Amarillo, Texas, in September 2015. Two security officers pursued a diamond-shaped object approximately four feet tall, traveling at 10–15 miles per hour. Witnesses gave conflicting color descriptions — some reported black, others silver, red, and blue. The object made no audible sound and displayed no visible propulsion system despite binocular observation. Pantex entered lockdown, though security analysts ultimately determined the object posed no threat to sensitive assets.

Pantex joins a growing list of nuclear weapons facilities with documented UAP encounters, alongside the 1967 incident that disabled ten ICBMs at Malmstrom Air Force Base. The recurrence of this pattern — unexplained objects specifically near nuclear infrastructure, across five decades and multiple administrations — is precisely the kind of institutional through-line that whistleblower testimony has pointed to directly.

The Green Fireballs of Los Alamos, 1949

One of the release's more unusual historical documents is a transcript from a 1949 conference at Los Alamos National Laboratory, where Manhattan Project physicists convened specifically to investigate recurring sightings of "green fireballs" near the facility. Attendees included scientists who had worked directly on the atomic bomb. The conference failed to attribute the phenomena to meteoric activity or any other natural cause; one astronomer present noted that luminous events of this kind had never been documented in meteorological literature. The sightings were left officially unexplained despite the concentration of scientific expertise in the room — a detail that undercuts any suggestion that credible witnesses only started taking UAP seriously in the modern disclosure era.

Civilian Orb Footage in the Northeast

Two civilian-recorded videos from 2024–2025 depict orb phenomena in the northeastern United States. An October 2024 clip titled "Orbs Over the Pond" shows a plasma-like sphere hovering roughly 2,700 feet above water for about 45 minutes, intermittently separating into smaller luminous points — one of which hovered just above the water surface in a way inconsistent with simple reflection. A July 2025 "Northeastern Orb Sighting," captured approximately 25 miles away, shows two bright lights moving in apparent synchronization, described by witnesses as silent and smooth, "in formation or tethered together."

New Analysis of an Apollo 17 Photograph

The release includes Space Shuttle imagery from STS-80 (1996) showing a triangular or cone-shaped object in low-Earth orbit during November–December operations. More significantly, the Department of War disclosed that it has opened an active investigation into the Apollo 17 photograph showing three bright dots in triangular formation above the lunar surface — a frame we noted in our coverage of the third PURSUE release as already under preliminary review. Officials now state that "new preliminary US government analysis suggests the image feature is potentially the result of a physical object in the scene," though no consensus exists on what that object might be. This is the first tranche to move that specific case from "flagged for review" to "under active investigation."

The CIA's Contribution to This Tranche

The CIA, whose first contribution to the public archive came with the third release in June, added further Cold War-era records alongside Pentagon and FBI material. The archive now spans documented cases from 1944 through 2026, with the government maintaining that roughly 40 percent of reported phenomena lack a reasonable conventional explanation. The range of contributing agencies — Defense, Energy, NASA, intelligence community partners, and State — reflects what officials describe as an interagency effort requiring coordination across dozens of offices reviewing tens of millions of records, many of which exist only on paper.

Still No Confirmed Extraterrestrial Evidence

The Pentagon maintained its consistent position across all four releases: none of the material constitutes confirmed evidence of extraterrestrial origin. AARO's own analysis has found no proof that any UAP case represents alien technology or non-human intelligence. Scientists and skeptics have noted that much of the footage remains ambiguous and potentially explainable through sensor artifacts, balloons, or advanced conventional military systems. Former AARO director Sean Kirkpatrick, who reviewed classified UAP records during his tenure, cautioned against expecting "bombshell revelations" from these releases, calling that expectation unrealistic given what he actually saw in the classified record.

Four Tranches In, the Pattern Holds

Four tranches in, the PURSUE pattern holds steady: real sensor data, real historical documents, real witness accounts from credible sources — and no single file that resolves the underlying question one way or the other. What's accumulating instead is a documented, decades-long institutional pattern: nuclear facilities drawing repeat encounters, historical scientific panels reaching the same "unexplained" conclusion as modern sensors, and now, for the first time, an official U.S. government statement suggesting an Apollo-era photograph may show a physical object rather than a camera artifact. Whether tranche five moves any of these cases from "unresolved" to something more definitive remains, as with every release so far, an open question the government says it intends to keep answering on a rolling basis.

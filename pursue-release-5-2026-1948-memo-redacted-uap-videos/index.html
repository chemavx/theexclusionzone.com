PURSUE Release 5: The 1948 Memo, the FBI's Triangles, and the Data Blacked Out of 16 Videos

The fifth PURSUE tranche landed on 7 August 2026: 41 records, 130 MB of documents and 16 videos. Inside, a SECRET 1948 memo in which the Air Force's Director of Intelligence warns that "silence on our part will not long be acceptable" — beside videos with the sensor data painted out.

On 3 November 1948, the Director of Intelligence of the United States Air Force sat down to write to Wright-Patterson Air Force Base about flying objects. His memorandum was stamped SECRET. It stayed that way for the better part of eight decades. It became public on 7 August 2026, in the fifth tranche of the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters — PURSUE — and it contains this sentence:

"Silence on our part will not long be acceptable."

The fifth tranche is 130 MB of documents and 513 MB of video. It is also, in one specific and measurable way, still silent.

What was actually released

The Department of War published Release 05 on 7 August 2026, three months after the first tranche of 8 May. The department's own page carries the announcement in plain terms — "The Department of War just released the fifth tranche of records under PURSUE" — and labels the section "RELEASE 05: CLEARED FOR RELEASE - AUG. 7, 2026". The department put the batch at 41 records drawn from the Department of War, the FBI, the CIA, the State Department and the Executive Office of the President, spanning material from 1950 to 2026.

For scale: the department's own record index, as it stood on 31 July 2026, listed 334 records across the first four tranches — 158 on 8 May, 64 on 22 May, 72 on 12 June and 40 on 10 July. Release 05 takes the running total to 375.

The running total is worth keeping in view, because each batch answers a different question. Release 03 stopped at 294 files and turned on the CIA's own admission and the Apollo lunar anomalies; this one adds the 1948 memorandum and the sixteen videos analysed below.

Two downloads sit on that page: a documents archive of 130 MB and a video archive of 513 MB. This article is based on both, plus the department's own captions.

The 1948 memorandum

The document catalogued as DOW-UAP-D100, filed under the title Air Materiel Command Report on UFOs, 1947–1948, runs to 245 pages. It opens with the memorandum quoted above.

The letterhead reads DEPARTMENT OF THE AIR FORCE / HEADQUARTERS UNITED STATES AIR FORCE / WASHINGTON. The date is 3 NOV 1948. The subject line is "Flying Object Incidents in the United States". It is addressed to the Commanding General, Air Materiel Command, Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, Dayton, Ohio. The SECRET stamps at the head and foot of the page have been struck through by hand. In the corner, the archival markings: document T-73017, page 238, declassification authority NND57563.

Its first paragraph establishes the chain: "By letter dated 30 December 1947 from the Director of Research and Development, Headquarters USAF, your Headquarters was required to establish Project 'SIGN'." Project SIGN was the Air Force's first formal UFO investigation, the direct ancestor of the effort that would later become Project Blue Book.

The second paragraph is the one that matters:

"The conclusion appears inescapable that some type of flying object has been observed. Identification and the origin of these objects is not discernible to this Headquarters. It is imperative, therefore, that efforts to determine whether these objects are of domestic or foreign origin must be increased until conclusive evidence is obtained. The needs of national defense require such evidence in order that appropriate countermeasures may be taken."

The third paragraph turns to the public:

"In addition to the imperative need for evidence to permit countermeasures, is the necessity of informing the public as to the status of the problem. To date there has been too little data to present to the public. The press, however, is about to take it into its own hands and demand to be told what we do or do not know about the situation. Silence on our part will not long be acceptable."

And the fourth asks Wright-Patterson a question that has aged strangely well: whether information "should be offered to the press or withheld until it is actively sought by the press."

It is signed "BY COMMAND OF THE CHIEF OF STAFF" by C. P. Cabell, Major General, USAF, Director of Intelligence, Office of Deputy Chief of Staff, Operations. Charles Pearre Cabell would later serve as Deputy Director of the CIA.

Read carefully, the memo is not a disclosure document. It is an internal instruction that treats unidentified objects as a national defence problem requiring countermeasures, and treats public communication as a media-management problem requiring a recommendation. Both readings are in the text. Neither is speculation.

Sixteen videos, and what has been removed from them

The video archive unpacks to 16 MP4 files, 546 MB in total, running 12 minutes and 58 seconds. The shortest clip is 2.8 seconds; the longest is 2 minutes 33 seconds. Resolutions range from 768×576 to 1920×1080. They are a mixed set: infrared and low-light sensor footage, some of it overhead, some of it looking out at a horizon, most of it carrying military tracking symbology — corner brackets, a tracking gate, a north cue.

They also carry something else. In five of the sixteen files, rectangular patches of pure black sit inside the picture itself, in the same position in every frame, over the places where a targeting sensor writes its data.

We checked this the same way in all sixteen files: sampling frames across each clip, measuring which pixels ever change, and then looking inside that active area for rectangles that stay pure black and perfectly still from the first frame to the last. Frame borders and letterboxing were excluded. Five files qualified.

The clearest example is the file named DOD_111887419.mp4, eleven seconds of infrared footage looking across a coastline. An elongated object hangs above the horizon; the sensor's cyan tracking gate is centred below it. Around the edges of the frame — top-left, top-centre, upper-right, mid-left, lower-right — the data fields are blacked out. So is a smaller rectangle immediately beneath the tracking gate.

In DOD_111887390.mp4, a hundred seconds of overhead infrared over an inhabited area beside a waterway, the same treatment: the tracking symbology and the north cue are left visible; the readouts are covered. The other three files with fixed black patches inside the frame are DOD_111887413.mp4, DOD_111887426.mp4 and DOD_111887460.mp4.

What sits in those fields on an unredacted feed is the ordinary business of a sensor: time, date, coordinates, altitude, sensor mode, slant range. It is exactly the information that would let an outside analyst place an event in space and time and check it against anything else — radar, air traffic, weather, another aircraft's tape.

This is not an accusation of bad faith. Redacting platform and position data is standard practice, and the reasons for it are usually operational rather than sinister. It is simply a fact about what was released: the pictures came out, and the numbers did not.

The FBI's contribution: drawings

The most-reproduced images from this tranche are not photographs. They are illustrations, and the government says so plainly in its own captions.

Among the featured records are eight FBI digital renderings, each described as "an artistic interpretation of a reported incident":

  • FBI-UAP-D025, "Airborne Triangle", above Bagram Air Force Base, Afghanistan, June 2002
  • FBI-UAP-D027, "Dark Translucent Triangle", near Colorado Springs, Colorado, October 2023
  • FBI-UAP-D029, "Dark Triangle with Lights", June or July 2011
  • FBI-UAP-D031, "Large Triangle with Red Lights", near Colorado Springs, 2023
  • FBI-UAP-D038, D039, D041 and D042, "Multiple Red Lights", western United States, 2026

Also featured is DOW-UAP-D098, a 1953 memorandum on the U.S. Naval Photographic Interpretation Center's assessment of a film said to show unidentified airborne objects.

The distinction matters more than it might appear. A rendering is a record of what a witness said, drawn afterwards by someone who was not there. It is evidence of testimony, not evidence of an object. Four of the eight renderings depict triangles, and two of those cluster around Colorado Springs in 2023 — which tells us something real about the consistency of what witnesses report, and nothing at all about what was in the air.

What the reporting adds, and what we could not check

Coverage of the tranche has centred on two cases we could not verify against the release index.

The first is an encounter in the Gulf of Oman in 2021, in which the crew of an AC-130J gunship is reported to have tracked roughly 25 unidentified objects during a live-fire exercise — described as cold, spherical, moving between 250 and 1,300 mph, and appearing to react to the aircraft's cannon fire. The second is the Bagram triangle: an FBI witness interview describing a silent equilateral triangle, reported as roughly 500 feet across, blacking out the stars at around 4:30 in the morning and moving at a steady 150 knots. The department's own caption for FBI-UAP-D025 places that incident in June 2002; the witness is reported to have come forward to the Pentagon in 2024.

We are flagging these as reporting rather than as documented fact for a specific reason. The index that pairs each video file with its case is served from the department's own site in a form that blocks automated retrieval, and we were unable to obtain the version covering Release 05. We can confirm what the sixteen video files contain, because we examined them. We cannot confirm which of them corresponds to the Gulf of Oman, and we will not guess.

What is left

Seventy-eight years separate the two halves of this release. In 1948 a two-star general wrote that the origin of these objects "is not discernible to this Headquarters", that the evidence must be pursued until it is conclusive, and that silence would not hold much longer. In 2026 that memorandum was published alongside sixteen videos, five of them with their timestamps and coordinates painted out, and eight drawings of things people say they saw.

Cabell's question to Wright-Patterson was whether the information should be offered to the press, or withheld until the press came looking. The answer, on the evidence of Release 05, is that it is being offered — with the verifiable parts removed.

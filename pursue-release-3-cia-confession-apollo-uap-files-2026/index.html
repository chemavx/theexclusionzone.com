PURSUE Release 3: The CIA Confession, Apollo Lunar Anomalies, and 294 Files That Raise More Questions Than They Answer

The Pentagon's third PURSUE release includes a 1992 CIA confession that spy planes caused over half of all UFO reports in the 1960s, active investigation of Apollo lunar photographs, and 294 declassified files spanning 82 years. The mystery deepens.

ChemaVX

The most intriguing federal disclosure yet arrived on June 12, 2026, when the Pentagon released its third tranche of declassified files through the Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE) — revealing 72 new documents that include the CIA's first contribution to public records, FBI investigator accounts of glowing orbs, and photographic evidence from lunar missions that triggered active government re-examination.

Three Releases in Five Weeks

PURSUE launched May 8, 2026, following President Trump's February directive ordering federal agencies to identify and declassify records connected to UFOs and alleged extraterrestrials. The first release contained 158 files; the second (May 22) added 64 more. The June 12 release brought the archive to 294 declassified files spanning 82 years — from 1944 World War II foo fighter reports to 2026 civilian and federal employee UAP encounters. The government website housing these materials, WAR.GOV/UFO, has received 1.7 billion hits worldwide since May 8. We covered the first two releases in depth: our analysis of what the first 222 files revealed and concealed, and our breakdown of the Pentagon's second release including Apollo astronaut testimony.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth framed the releases as demonstrating "unprecedented transparency," while Pentagon spokesperson Sean Parnell stated the Department of War and agency partners are "actively working on the next release of UAP files" on a rolling basis.

What Release 3 Actually Contains

The expanded archive now includes 75 military sensor videos across CENTCOM, INDOPACOM, AFRICOM, and domestic operations. Release 3 adds genuinely new content categories absent from previous disclosures: the CIA's first contribution (18 historical Cold War records), FBI civilian witness videos shot in 2024 and 2025, multi-agency Colorado Springs incident files, and evidence triggering active Department of War investigation into Apollo lunar photography. A June 5, 2026 report signed by Dr. Jon Kosloski, director of AARO, noted that 40% of reported phenomena lack reasonable explanation and remain unresolved.

Witness testimony now includes accounts from federal employees describing orange "mother" orbs allegedly ejecting smaller red orbs at test ranges, corroborated by FBI interviews conducted in 2023 and 2024. One intelligence officer described "countless orange orbs swarming in all directions" near a military facility in late 2025. Another report documented five federal agents witnessing formation flight with perfect coordination, smaller orbs appearing to separate from a larger primary source multiple times.

Specific Objects and Technical Specifications

Release 3 includes detailed descriptions absent from civilian reports. A September 2023 sighting documented a bronze metallic object estimated at 130–195 feet in length that "materialized out of a bright light in the sky" before "disappearing instantaneously," with five federal employee witnesses providing corroborating accounts. An October 2024 video titled "Orbs Over the Pond" captured a luminous object hovering 2,700 feet away above water for approximately 45 minutes, described as a "plasma-like sphere" intermittently changing shape and luminosity, with smaller luminous points separating from the primary source.

A July 2025 "Northeastern Orb Sighting" showed two bright lights moving silently and smoothly through the sky, appearing to travel in tandem before merging as they moved out of sight. Military sensor footage from prior releases includes a diamond-shaped UAP tracked at 434 knots over Greece in January 2024, visible only via Short-Wave Infrared sensor and invisible to all other detection methods. Multiple 90-degree turns executed at 80 mph just above ocean surface remain aerodynamically unexplained.

The CIA Confession: How Spy Planes Became UFOs

The document behind this admission is the CIA History Staff study The Central Intelligence Agency and Overhead Reconnaissance: The U-2 and OXCART Programs, 1954-1974, written in 1992 by Gregory Pedlow and Donald Welzenbach. It records that U-2 and later OXCART high-altitude flights accounted for more than half of all UFO reports from the late 1950s through the 1960s, with Project Blue Book giving the public false explanations to protect the classified programs.

Correction (16 September 2026): this article originally described that study as "released publicly for the first time" in Release 3. That was wrong, and the error matters more than the detail it gets wrong. A redacted copy reached researcher Jeffrey Richelson in 2002; his 2005 FOIA request to review the redactions produced the version with the Area 51 references restored, which the CIA released in 2013 and the National Security Archive published on 15 August that year. The specific figure is older still: CIA historian Gerald K. Haines set it out in A Die-Hard Issue: CIA's Role in the Study of UFOs, 1947-90, published in the agency's own journal Studies in Intelligence in 1997, which states that Blue Book investigators "were careful, however, not to reveal the true cause" and that this "led the Air Force to make misleading and deceptive statements to the public." Release 3 placed these records inside the PURSUE archive; it did not declassify them. The distinction changes the conclusion: the deception was not confessed in 2026 but decades earlier, in public, and almost nobody read it.

This is not a minor footnote. It means the U.S. government's official UFO investigation program was, for at least a decade, knowingly attributing real classified aircraft to unexplained phenomena — and the same government assuring the public there was nothing to see. The Robertson Panel (1952–1953), also included in Release 3, reveals the policy behind this approach: the CIA-convened scientific advisory panel recommended official "debunking" of UFOs to "strip the UFO subject of its mystery," warning that "morbid national psychology" around UFOs could be exploited by adversaries. The CIA's own Cold War strategy was to make the public stop taking UAP seriously — context that is impossible to ignore when evaluating any official position on the subject.

Apollo Mission Records and Lunar Anomalies

Release 3 includes previously classified Apollo mission materials. A Gemini 7 transcript from December 1965 records astronaut Frank Borman reporting a "bogey" to Houston and estimating "hundreds of little particles" at approximately four miles distance. Apollo 12 crew audio from December 1969 medical debriefings captures Pete Conrad, Richard Gordon, and Alan Bean describing "streaks of lights" observed while sleeping during the lunar mission — NASA had classified these tapes, attributing the effects to internal visual phenomena.

Most significantly, five Apollo 12 photographs (1969) with highlighted areas showing "unidentified phenomena" above the lunar horizon appear in the archive. One Apollo 17 photograph (1972) shows three dots in triangular formation in the lunar sky. The Department of War has opened an active investigation into the Apollo 17 image, with documentation referencing "new preliminary US government analysis" as of June 2026 — the first time a specific Apollo photograph has triggered formal renewed government examination.

Colorado Springs: The Object With Fish-Scale Panels

A 2022 incident report from Fort Carson describes five soldiers who watched a white, potato-shaped object hovering near Cheyenne Mountain in daylight. The object featured "distinct edges" composed of "articulating fish scales or panels," the soldiers reported, with "no shadow" visible and the object appearing to cloak in the moment they briefly looked away. An intelligence partner analysis assessed with "low confidence" that sunlight backscattering from snow on Cheyenne Mountain might illuminate low-altitude clouds — the case remains officially unresolved.

Scientific and Skeptical Reception

Dr. Avi Loeb, Harvard astrophysicist now heading the newly created UAP Science Advisory Council announced concurrently with Release 3, called this disclosure "the most intriguing thus far," noting that 40% of reported phenomena remain unexplained and that "more data needs to be collected and analyzed in order to figure out the origin of these Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena."

Skeptical reaction was pointed. Scientist Mick West questioned whether these videos represent the best Pentagon holdings, noting the released footage is "not amazing." Astrophysicist David Whitehouse reviewed the materials and found "optical artefacts, fuzzy blobs, and light smears. Some obviously balloons. No hint, no evidence whatsoever of anything artificial and alien." Former AARO director Sean Kirkpatrick warned that without analysis or context, the releases "will only serve to fuel more speculation, conspiracy and armchair pseudoscience."

Some UAP researchers have drawn connections between the orb descriptions and whistleblower David Grusch's Capitol Hill testimony — though it's worth noting that such interpretations go beyond what the documents themselves establish. The files describe observed phenomena; they do not confirm the nature or origin of what was observed. Grusch's full testimony and its institutional implications are examined in our investigation of the June 9 Capitol press conference.

What the Files Do Not Contain

The releases provide no confirmed evidence of extraterrestrial life or technology. The Pentagon explicitly states these are "unresolved cases" where "the government is unable to make a definitive determination on the nature of the observed phenomena." No confirmed recovered technology, biological specimens, or wreckage documentation appears in any release. Pentagon notes many videos lacked "substantiated chain of custody," acknowledging they could have been altered at some point.

International Context

The U.S. releases are prompting parallel action internationally. Canada's Office of the Chief Science Advisor released the Sky Canada Project report (June 2025) recommending establishment of a formal federal UAP reporting system. France's GEIPAN has operated within the national space agency since 1977. Chile's SEFAA operates within civil aviation authority. The U.S. now leads institutionally with AARO (established 2022) and the newly announced UAP Science Advisory Council — though the gap between institutional infrastructure and actual disclosure remains the central unresolved question.

294 Files, 82 Years, and the Mystery Deepens

The PURSUE system has consolidated 294 declassified files spanning from World War II to 2026 across five agencies, proving the U.S. government has systematically tracked unexplained objects with extraordinary capabilities for eight decades. Release 3 significantly broadened historical context with CIA Cold War records and triggered active Pentagon re-examination of Apollo lunar photography — and revealed that for at least a decade, the official UFO investigation was knowingly misleading the public to protect classified programs.

Whether these disclosures ultimately advance scientific understanding or expand the mystery depends on forthcoming higher-quality data — and whether the government releases its most sensitive sensor material, which AARO technical specifications suggest must exist but has not yet appeared in public archives. As Loeb stated: "It's like a detective story. And the best is yet to come."

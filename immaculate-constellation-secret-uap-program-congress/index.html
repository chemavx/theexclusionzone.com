Immaculate Constellation: The Secret UAP Program Congress Still Can't Confirm

In November 2024, a whistleblower handed Congress documents describing a secret UAP program called Immaculate Constellation. The Pentagon denied it exists. Nineteen months and 294 declassified files later, the question remains exactly where it started.

In November 2024, a Pentagon whistleblower handed Congress a 12-page document describing a secret UAP surveillance program called Immaculate Constellation. The Pentagon denied it existed. Nineteen months and three rounds of declassified files later, no government source has confirmed or definitively refuted it — and the program's name still doesn't appear anywhere in the 294 files released through PURSUE.

The November 2024 Hearing

On November 13, 2024, the House Oversight and Accountability Committee held a joint hearing featuring four witnesses: Rear Admiral Tim Gallaudet (retired Navy), Luis Elizondo (former Department of Defense official), Michael Shellenberger (investigative journalist and founder of the independent outlet Public, known for his reporting on government whistleblowers), and Michael Gold (former NASA associate administrator). The hearing continued a bipartisan congressional effort to investigate UAP that gained momentum after retired Navy Commander David Fravor's 2004 Tic Tac encounter — documented in our investigation of the USS Nimitz incident — and intensified following David Grusch's 2023 testimony.

The Immaculate Constellation Document

Shellenberger delivered a 12-page whistleblower report to Congress alleging the existence of an "Unacknowledged Special Access Program" called Immaculate Constellation. The document claims this Pentagon program consolidates UAP observations collected from low Earth orbit, the upper atmosphere, military and civilian aircraft, and maritime environments, using infrared, forward-looking infrared, full-motion video, and still photography. Shellenberger stated multiple independent sources corroborated the program's existence and that he had verified the whistleblower's identity as a current or former government official.

The whistleblower report describes specific encounters recorded in military intelligence databases. One incident involved multiple orbs that surrounded and boxed in an F-22 aircraft during patrol, with one UAP maintaining an exact spatial relationship with the fighter jet's cockpit despite evasive maneuvers. Another documented a Navy aircraft carrier crew observing a small orange-red sphere descending to 100-200 yards above the flight deck, its surface appearing "roiling like the surface of the sun" without illuminating surrounding surfaces. Shellenberger also described footage of a white orb emerging from the ocean approximately 20 miles off Kuwait's coast, filmed from a helicopter, later joined by a second orb.

Pentagon Denial and a Documented Pattern

DOD spokesperson Sue Gough issued a statement denying any such program exists. Shellenberger countered by documenting a historical pattern in which the Pentagon initially denies UAP-related records, then acknowledges them after FOIA appeals are filed. Researcher John Greenewald reported 27 years of increasingly restrictive government responses: the Navy claimed in March 2022 it found no additional UAP videos beyond three publicly released ones, then admitted in September 2022 that further videos and photographs existed but were classified.

Congressional Skepticism About AARO

Chairwoman Nancy Mace criticized AARO's March 2024 historical report as "error-ridden" with poor referencing. Former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense Christopher Mellon called it "the most error-ridden and unsatisfactory government report I can recall reading." AARO had dismissed reverse-engineering claims as "circular reporting" lacking evidence — a position we examined in detail in our investigation of AARO's eight-decade review. The office's budget remains classified despite taxpayer funding.

Witness Testimony on Crash Retrieval

When questioned directly, Elizondo stated under oath that the U.S. government has conducted "secret UAP crash retrieval programs" designed "to identify and reverse engineer alien craft." He noted government employees have received compensation for injuries sustained from UAP incidents. When pressed on the apparent contradiction of denying recovered craft while paying injury claims related to them, Elizondo responded: "That is a great question. That is why I think we are here again."

Retaliation and a Culture of Suppression

Elizondo testified that a "small cadre" within government created a "culture of suppression and intimidation," involving unwarranted criminal investigations, harassment, and credibility destruction against whistleblowers. He stated the Pentagon's public affairs office employs a psychological operations officer as the singular point of contact for UAP inquiries from citizens and media. Gallaudet described a 2024 AARO meeting he characterized as an "hours-long influence operation" attempting to discredit valid UAP reports — testimony that echoes Grusch's later description of "administrative terrorism" used against whistleblowers in his June 2026 Capitol testimony.

Historical UAP Morphology

The whistleblower report documented six primary UAP shapes encountered from 1991 to 2022: spheres/orbs, discs/saucers, ovals/tic-tacs, triangles, boomerang/arrowhead shapes, and irregular/organic forms. Some incidents included documented biological effects on witnesses, including unease, electronic device malfunctions, and physical sensations, while other craft types showed no such effects.

A Constitutional Question, Not Just a UAP One

Admiral Gallaudet emphasized that withholding UAP information from Congress "infringes on your legislative and oversight responsibilities to such an extent that's very concerning," describing it as potentially "the greatest issue of our time" being hidden from elected representatives. The statute requiring notification of Special Access Programs (10 USC § 119) appears, according to testimony, to have been circumvented through a mechanism where the White House directs the DOD to run programs in a way that avoids Title 10 compliance requirements.

The credibility of the testimony rests partly on the witnesses' backgrounds. Elizondo managed a White House special access program for the National Security Council. Gallaudet commanded the Naval Meteorology and Oceanography Command. Gold led NASA's UAP Independent Study Team. These are career government and military officials with significant clearances, not fringe figures, testifying publicly about alleged suppression efforts.

The pattern predates all of them. the Wilson-Davis memo, notes of a 2002 conversation with a former Deputy Director of the Defense Intelligence Agency, describes an admiral being refused access to a program he held the clearance to see — and being told the refusal itself was not something he could appeal.

The classification pattern raises its own questions. If these materials pose no national security threat, why does even naming the program trigger concerns among members of Congress? Representative Jared Moskowitz's observation during the hearing — "you can't talk about fight club if there's no fight club" — captured the basic logical tension: restricting discussion of a program is difficult to reconcile with the claim that the program doesn't exist.

What Changed — and What Didn't — Through 2026

Nineteen months after the hearing, none of the three PURSUE releases — totaling 294 declassified files as of June 2026 — has mentioned Immaculate Constellation by name, confirmed its existence, or formally denied it beyond the original 2024 Pentagon statement. AARO's official position remains that no government investigation has found evidence of extraterrestrial technology. Grusch's June 2026 Capitol testimony described program structures — special access programs operating above congressional notification thresholds — that align with what Shellenberger described in 2024, though Grusch did not name Immaculate Constellation specifically in public testimony.

The gap between alleged program and confirmed record remains exactly where it was in November 2024: a documented whistleblower complaint, a flat institutional denial, and no public resolution either way.

A Gap in Congressional Notification

The November 2024 hearing introduced documentary evidence of an alleged secret UAP program into the congressional record while exposing apparent gaps in congressional notification compliance. Whether Immaculate Constellation represents advanced human technology, foreign adversarial capability, or something else, the pattern established by 2026 is consistent: bipartisan congressional concern, persistent institutional denial, and a disclosure process that — three PURSUE releases later — still hasn't addressed the specific claim that started this debate.
